Have an overall story. Use examples whenever possible. Use consistent, paper-long running examples if possible. Examples can provide motivation, introduce the problem, help understand the solution, etc.
Cite the most relevant related works in the introduction to introduce the research gap and compare against your solution. Then, provide a bulleted list of your contributions.
Ensure that there is flow among paragraphs and sentences.
Ensure that all claims are backed up using citations or theorems/results within your paper.
Do not use “majority”, “most” type of words without ensuring you can back up that claim.
Do not use “very”, “strongly” type of unnecessary and informal words.
Do not form vague statements.
Check that every sentence is useful. Each paragraph and sentence must have a goal. If you remove a sentence and the meaning is not lost, that sentence should be removed.
Use active rather than passive.
Do not simply provide technical details. All technical details must be followed or preceded by intuitive explanations (potentially using examples).
Always group related work, and make sure you do not only say bad things about them.
Ensure that all abbreviations are defined at their first use.
Ensure that you adhere to all the rules of submission (e.g., paper format, page limit, anonymous submission, appendix and bibliography limits)
You must know all such submission details and dates when you are communicating with your advisor.
Make your bibliography proper, clean, consistent, and short. This means, go into your .bib file, edit the entries such that:
Editors are removed
URLs and DOIs are removed
Notes are removed
For Journals, the type is @article, and in addition to the journal name, you need to list volume/issue and pages.
For Conferences, the type is @inproceedings, and the conference name only appears in short form without year (e.g., instead of “In Proceedings of the 36th ACM Conference on Computer and Communications Security, 2021”, booktitle should just say “ACM CCS”). If you will save space, you may remove pages from conference publications (but not journal).
Always ensure that the citation contains the year. If it is a link, it should contain the last access date.
Most of the time, you do not need the publisher.
