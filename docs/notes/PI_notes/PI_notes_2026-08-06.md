## Note 6. Introduction, the definition of one-shot

**Location.** `intro.tex`, the sentence beginning "We use one-shot in this sense
throughout".

**What the PI wrote.**

> hard to understand sentence, humanize

**Why the comment was made.** The sentence asks the reader to parse three layers
at once. It sets a negative against a positive, "not as a claim ... but as the
statement", and the thing being denied is itself a negative, "the parties never
communicate again". Both halves are nominalized, so the reader holds "a claim"
and "the statement" in mind instead of holding the two meanings. Splitting the
contrast into two short sentences removes every layer.

**Replacement text.**

```latex
In this paper, one-shot does not mean the parties stop communicating. It means that
no intermediate training artifact is ever exposed.
```

**This sentence is load-bearing and its content must not drift.** It is the
paper's defence against a reviewer who reads "one-shot" as a claim of silence
after training. Key generation precedes the protocol and serving costs traffic on
every query, and Section V accounts for both. The replacement keeps both halves
of the definition and changes only the sentence structure.

## Note 9. Introduction, differentiate on fine-tuning, not only on disclosure

**Location.** `intro.tex`, the encrypted-learning paragraph and the paragraph
beginning "Applying both still leaves one artifact exposed".

**What the PI wrote.**

> I think you should differentiate your work also through fine-tuning. poseidon

**Why the comment was made.** This is a positioning note, not a wording fix. As

after the citation group from note 8.

```latex
That cost scales with the network being trained: POSEIDON trains a three-layer
network of sixty-four neurons per layer on handwritten digits at ten parties in
$1.4$ hours~\cite{sav2021poseidon}, and its successors lower the constant without
changing what sets it. Adapting a pretrained transformer by this route is out of
reach, and that is the case this paper addresses.
```

## Note 16. Introduction, the contribution list. CLOSES P2

**Location.** `intro.tex`, the itemized contribution list.

**Source.** Not a margin comment. Applied under the PI's standing instruction of
11:54, "I would also humanize the rest and above". This is P2 in the preemptive
list.

**Why it needed the pass.** Each item runs five to seven lines and mixes three
things: what the component is, why it is needed, and what it denies an adversary.
The middle one is the story element. A reader scanning the contribution list wants
to know what was built, and can take the reason on trust until the section that
argues it.

Two rules from the `academic-ste` register also bind here and were not being
followed. A contribution is an artifact a reader can point at, never an activity,
so the headings should be noun phrases naming things. And the third item wrote
"We give an estimator", which puts the authors rather than the component in the
subject position.

**Replacement text.**

```latex
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A split trainable unit.} Each client keeps its own adapter and never
  transmits it. Only the classifier head is federated, because a client can improve
  its own features alone but cannot recognise a class it has never seen. No adapter
  aggregate exists, so a coalition of all but one client has nothing to subtract
  from (\cref{sec:split}).
\item \textbf{A serving path that never decrypts.} The querying client encrypts its
  own features, so the serving party cannot read the query. The serving party
  applies the encrypted head and takes the argmax under encryption, and a quorum
  switches the result to the querier's key. Only a label leaves the protocol, and a
  label denies the linear solve that logits would permit (\cref{sec:serving}).
\item \textbf{A selection rule that runs under encryption.} Two arrangements are
  servable at the same cost, and which one is better depends on the task. Each
  client scoring both on its own held-out data and voting estimates the wrong
  quantity, and selects wrongly whenever the two differ. The estimator given here
  corrects that, needs no fitted threshold, and reveals only which arrangement won
  (\cref{sec:selection}).
\end{itemize}
```

**What changed.** About one hundred and sixty words against two hundred and
twenty. Sentences are shorter and each carries one idea. The headings now name
artifacts rather than activities. The first person is gone from the third item.
Every cross-reference is unchanged.

**If the PI prefers the original headings**, revert only the three bold phrases.
The bodies stand on their own and none of them depends on the heading wording.
