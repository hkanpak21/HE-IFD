\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented HE-IFD, a one-shot federated distillation in which the server's only operation on client
contributions is a linear aggregation computed under multiparty homomorphic encryption at multiplicative depth one. The
construction gives the contributions cryptographic, lossless privacy at the communication budget of a single
round, and because the feature extractor is frozen and stays outside the encrypted combination, the protocol
and its cost are unchanged from small networks to frozen pretrained backbones. The element that makes a
one-shot linear aggregation succeed under heterogeneity is the shared loss basin, a cheap and possibly weak
common initialization whose purpose is to align the clients' updates rather than to classify well. Across
vision and language tasks the method yields a common model far stronger than any client's own and close to a
centralized one, a real multiparty CKKS implementation confirms the encrypted aggregation at a few megabytes
per round, and the accuracy cost of differentially private basin summaries is negligible across the
practical range of privacy budgets. Extending the approach
to actively malicious participants, where verifiable homomorphic encryption~\cite{viand2023verifiable,atapoor2024vfhe}
could certify that the server computed the aggregation honestly, and tightening the analysis of what the
released model itself reveals, are natural directions for future work.
