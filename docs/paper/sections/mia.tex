The cryptographic and differential-privacy guarantees of \cref{sec:privacy} concern the contributions and
the basin summaries, not the final global model, which every party obtains in the clear after decryption.
A complete privacy account must therefore ask how much an adversary can infer about a client's data from
that released model. We study this through membership-inference attacks~\cite{shokri2017membership,yeom2018privacy,salem2019ml,carlini2022membership},
which test whether a given example was part of the training data, applied across the surfaces an adversary
could realistically observe in our protocol, including the federated-distillation-specific
surfaces~\cite{yang2023fdleaks,wang2024graddiff,gu2023ldia,jagielski2023students}. This section reports
that study.
