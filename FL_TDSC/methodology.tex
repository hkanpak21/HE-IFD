\subsection{HE-IFD: One-Shot Distillation Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

This section presents the HE-IFD protocol in full generality. The framework applies to any architecture that admits a block decomposition with HE-compatible operations; architecture-specific instantiations are deferred to Section~\ref{sec:architecture}.

\subsubsection{System and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat_binding}

\par\noindent\textbf{Parties and trust.} We consider a cross-silo federated learning setting with $N$ clients and a central server. Each client $i$ holds a private dataset $\mathcal{D}_i$ that is never shared or transmitted. All parties are honest-but-curious: they execute the protocol faithfully but pool every observation to attempt to recover what the protocol intends to hide. The adversary may corrupt the server together with up to $N{-}1$ clients and merge their views; privacy of any client's input is preserved as long as at least one client is honest. Verifying that a semi-honest server has actually performed the homomorphic operations it claims is outside the present scope and is the natural extension toward verifiable HE discussed alongside the malicious-client surface in the Future Work section.

\par\noindent\textbf{Multiparty CKKS at $t{=}N$.} The cryptographic primitive is the multiparty variant of CKKS in the threshold instantiation of Mouchet et al.~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty}, as available in Lattigo~\cite{lattigo}. Distributed key generation produces a single collective public key $\mathsf{pk}$ and per-client secret-key shares $\mathsf{sk}_i$, with the threshold for decryption set to $t{=}N$: all $N$ shares are required to recover any plaintext. The choice $t{=}N$ over $t < N$ is deliberate. Any $t < N$ admits a coalition of $t{-}1$ semi-honest clients who can decrypt every aggregate the protocol exposes, including per-row teacher logits, which under the present threat model directly enables subtraction attacks against the remaining honest client. The cost of $t{=}N$ is a liveness surface (any one client can refuse to participate in the decryption round); this is standard in the multiparty-FHE literature and is not a privacy concern.

\par\noindent\textbf{Binding invariant on threshold decryption.} The central property of the protocol's threat model is the following: the only ciphertext ever subjected to threshold decryption, or to collective key-switching to a per-client target key, is the final trained student's parameter vector $\theta_E$. No per-client logit ciphertext, no aggregated ensemble target, and no auxiliary encrypted scalar is decrypted at any stage. The binding invariant is part of the threat model, not a property of the protocol implementation: it constrains what the protocol is allowed to release rather than what it can compute. Without it, a coalition of $N{-}1$ clients who know their own contributions and observe a decrypted aggregate can subtract their inputs and recover the honest client's contribution exactly. With it, the only adversary-visible plaintext is $\theta_E$, which is a non-linear function of every client's private inputs through the distillation training and therefore admits no closed-form subtraction attack on any single client.

\par\noindent\textbf{Plaintext student weights during training.} The student weights themselves are not encrypted during training: the warm-started student $\theta_0^\star$ and the per-iteration plaintext student state used to compute gradients are never encrypted. What carries cryptographic protection is a separate encrypted accumulator $\langle\Delta\rangle = \sum_t \eta \langle g_t\rangle$ holding the teacher-induced refinement, computed from the encrypted teacher signal $\langle T_i(\mathcal P)\rangle$ applied to the plaintext student state. At release time the two are composed: $\langle\theta_E\rangle = \langle\theta_0^\star\rangle + \langle\Delta\rangle$, threshold-decrypted, and shipped to the clients. The ciphertext-times-plaintext versus ciphertext-times-ciphertext distinction is therefore real but already optimised: CT$\times$PT is the dominant arithmetic throughout the per-step update, while CT$\times$CT appears only in the depth-bounded ensemble target construction (Section~\ref{sec:phase2}), which is at most three multiplicative levels deep and runs once per protocol execution. The binding invariant is preserved because the only ciphertext ever submitted to threshold decryption is the composed end-state $\langle\theta_E\rangle$, never an intermediate $\langle\Delta\rangle$ or $\langle T_i(\mathcal P)\rangle$ in isolation.

\par\noindent\textbf{Adversary's view.} Under the binding invariant and the IND-CPA security of CKKS, the adversary's view (server in coalition with $N{-}1$ clients) partitions into three disjoint subsets. The encrypted subset consists of the per-client teacher logit ciphertexts $\langle T_i(\mathcal P)\rangle$, any encrypted scalars $\langle\alpha_i\rangle$, the encrypted ensemble target $\widetilde Y$, and the encrypted accumulator $\langle\Delta\rangle$ at every step of training, all computationally indistinguishable from uniformly random ring elements. The plaintext-metadata subset consists of the public probe $\mathcal P$ itself, ciphertext sizes and counts, protocol timing, and any per-client public sizes that are disclosed by transport; this subset reveals only protocol structure, not data. The released subset consists of the threshold-decrypted student $\theta_E$ alone. The released-student leakage is exactly the lower bound that no collaborative-training protocol can avoid: any output usable as a model is observable to its recipients through white-box or statistical-query access. The contribution of cryptographic instantiation is to hold the adversary's view at exactly this floor, with nothing above it; the inference-attack surface that this floor closes off relative to plaintext distillation is reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:bg_server_inference} and is not duplicated here.

\par\noindent\textbf{All-zeros amplification and its defence.} The binding invariant prevents direct decryption attacks but does not rule out a derived form, utility-coercion-into-privacy-amplification: if $N{-}1$ colluding clients upload all-zero logit ciphertexts, the encrypted ensemble target collapses to a multiple of the single honest client's logits, the released student becomes a single-teacher distillation of that honest teacher, and the statistical-query floor on $\theta_E$ rises silently. The attack does not breach the invariant; it inflates the unavoidable lower bound. Two independent knobs defend against it. (P1)~Each client trains its teacher $T_i$ on $\mathcal{D}_i$ with DP-SGD at a per-client budget $(\varepsilon_T, \delta_T)$; by the post-processing theorem, $T_i(\mathcal P)$ inherits this budget and so does the released student, and the all-zeros amplification cannot reduce a client-local floor that is already established before any server-side aggregation. (P2)~Each client may add per-row Gaussian noise $\eta_i \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_P^2 I)$ to its logit vector before encryption. In the honest case this averages as $\sigma_P/\sqrt N$ at the ensemble target so utility loss is mild; under the all-zeros amplification it surfaces at full magnitude and contributes an additional $(\varepsilon_P, \delta_P)$ layer that takes effect only in the worst case. The composition of (P1) and (P2) follows the standard Gaussian-DP RDP accountant. The claim made by the methodology is structural: the protocol admits both knobs while keeping the binding invariant; concrete budget values belong to the experimental section.

\par\noindent\textbf{Out-of-scope adversaries.} A malicious server that deviates from the prescribed homomorphic computation is out of scope; the natural extension is verifiable HE. A malicious client that crafts adversarial logit ciphertexts (encrypted-feature poisoning) or biased payloads (model poisoning under encryption) is out of scope; this is a robustness concern that does not breach privacy under the binding invariant and is taken up explicitly in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}, where the absence of a low-depth HE-compatible robust aggregator is named as an open problem. Network-level adversaries (denial of service, traffic analysis, side-channels of the underlying HE library, timing leaks beyond ciphertext sizes) are likewise out of scope.

% \label{sec:overview}

\par\noindent\textbf{Protocol overview.} HE-IFD instantiates an encrypted central federated distillation (CFD) protocol on a public probe set $\mathcal P$ of size $|\mathcal P|$, with the threshold-key structure of Section~\ref{sec:threat_binding} as its security backbone. The protocol runs in five phases, formalised in Algorithm~\ref{alg:cfd}. In Phase~0, the $N$ clients jointly run the distributed key generation (DKG) of the multiparty CKKS construction once, producing the collective public key $\mathsf{pk}$ and the secret-key shares $\{\mathsf{sk}_i\}$. In Phase~1, each client trains a local teacher $T_i$ on its private data, evaluates $T_i$ on the public probe, and uploads the encrypted logit tensor $\langle T_i(\mathcal P)\rangle$ together with a single encrypted confidence scalar $\langle\alpha_i\rangle$. This is the only client-to-server message, and it is one-shot. In Phase~2, the server performs an optional plaintext warm-start on the public probe to obtain $\theta_0^\star$, then constructs the encrypted ensemble target $\widetilde Y$ from the per-client logit ciphertexts (Section~\ref{sec:ensemble_target}) and runs encrypted SGD against $\widetilde Y$ for $E_2$ epochs (Section~\ref{sec:linear_accumulator}). In Phase~3, the server broadcasts the encrypted final student $\langle\theta_E\rangle$ to the clients, who execute a single round of collective key-switching (Section~\ref{sec:phase3}) and recover $\theta_E$ in plaintext. In Phase~4, clients optionally fine-tune $\theta_E$ on their local data to obtain a personalised $\theta_{E,i}$, requiring no further communication.

The binding invariant of Section~\ref{sec:threat_binding} governs what may be threshold-decrypted: only $\theta_E$ is ever released in plaintext. No per-client logit ciphertext, no intermediate ensemble target, and no per-step accumulator state crosses the decryption boundary. The student forward pass during Phase~2 runs on a plaintext copy of the current weights, with only the gradient contribution lifted into ciphertext and added to a linear accumulator on $\langle\theta\rangle$. This construction keeps the per-step encrypted depth bounded by three multiplicative levels (Section~\ref{sec:linear_accumulator}) and confines the depth-3 ciphertext-by-ciphertext arithmetic of the ensemble target construction to a single pre-training computation. The protocol therefore fits inside a single CKKS level chain at $\log N \in \{14, 15\}$ with no bootstrapping for any cell of the experimental grid (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\begin{algorithm}[t!]
\small
\caption{Encrypted CFD (one round)}
\label{alg:cfd}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $N$ clients with private datasets $\mathcal{D}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{D}_N$; public probe $(\mathcal P, y_{\mathcal P})$; student architecture $S$; multiparty CKKS public key $\mathsf{pk}$ and shares $\{\mathsf{sk}_i\}$ from DKG; learning rate $\eta$; epochs $E_1, E_2$
\ENSURE Plaintext student $\theta_E$ at each client
\STATE \textbf{--- Phase 1: client-side (parallel, local) ---}
\FOR{each client $i = 1, \ldots, N$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \STATE Train teacher $T_i$ on $\mathcal{D}_i$ (DP-SGD if defence (P1) is active)
    \STATE Compute logits $L_i \leftarrow T_i(\mathcal{P})$ and confidence $\alpha_i \leftarrow \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}}[\max\sigma(T_i)]$
    \STATE (Optional defence (P2)) $L_i \leftarrow L_i + \eta_i$, $\eta_i \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_P^2 I)$
    \STATE Encrypt $\langle L_i\rangle, \langle\alpha_i\rangle$ under $\mathsf{pk}$; upload to server \hfill\COMMENT{one-shot}
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{--- Phase 2a: warm-start ($\alpha$ variant) ---}
\STATE $\theta_0^\star \leftarrow$ plaintext SGD on $(\mathcal{P}, y_{\mathcal P})$ for $E_1$ epochs
\STATE \textbf{--- Phase 2b: ensemble target (depth $\leq 3$) ---}
\STATE $\widetilde Y \leftarrow \sum_i \langle\alpha_i^\beta\rangle \cdot \langle L_i\rangle$, $\langle V_k\rangle$ per-row, $r_k \leftarrow 1 + \lambda\langle V_k\rangle$ \hfill\COMMENT{Section~\ref{sec:ensemble_target}}
\STATE \textbf{--- Phase 2c: encrypted SGD (linear accumulator) ---}
\STATE $\langle\theta\rangle \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}(\theta_0^\star)$
\FOR{$t = 1, \ldots, E_2 \cdot |\mathcal{P}|/B$}
    \STATE plaintext student forward on a probe minibatch with current $\theta_t$
    \STATE compute encrypted gradient $\langle g_t\rangle$ from $\widetilde Y$ and the plaintext student state
    \STATE $\langle\theta\rangle \mathrel{+}= \eta \cdot \langle g_t\rangle$ \hfill\COMMENT{depth $\leq 3$ per step}
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{--- Phase 3: collective key-switch ---}
\STATE Server broadcasts $\langle\theta_E\rangle$; each client contributes its key-switching share $\to \theta_E$
\STATE \textbf{--- Phase 4: optional personalisation ---}
\FOR{each client $i = 1, \ldots, N$}
    \STATE (Optional) fine-tune $\theta_E$ on $\mathcal{D}_i$ to obtain $\theta_{E,i}$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsubsection{Phase~1: Client-Side Teacher Training and Encrypted Upload}
\label{sec:phase1}

Each client $i$ trains a teacher $T_i$ on its private data $\mathcal{D}_i$ using standard supervised learning, optionally with DP-SGD at a per-client budget $(\varepsilon_T, \delta_T)$ when defence (P1) of Section~\ref{sec:threat_binding} is engaged. The teacher is then evaluated on the public probe $\mathcal{P}$ to produce a logit tensor $L_i = T_i(\mathcal{P}) \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{P}|\times C}$ and a per-client confidence scalar $\alpha_i = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}}[\max \sigma(T_i)]$. Under the optional defence (P2), each client adds per-row Gaussian noise $\eta_i \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma_P^2 I)$ to $L_i$ before encryption; the noise enters the plaintext before any CKKS encoding so that it is preserved through the encryption map and remains a properly-calibrated DP perturbation on the released student under the standard Gaussian-DP RDP accountant.

The encrypted upload consists of one CKKS ciphertext bundle per client: the logit tensor $\langle L_i\rangle$ packed into $\lceil |\mathcal{P}|\cdot C / N_{\text{slots}}\rceil$ slots and the scalar $\langle\alpha_i\rangle$ in a single slot. This is the only client-to-server message in the protocol. No feature-extraction tensors, no per-channel normalisation statistics, and no model weights cross the channel.

The public probe is plaintext on both client and server in the $\alpha$ variant of the experimental grid: it was held out before any private partitioning, is statistically independent of every client's private data, and is part of the protocol assumption rather than the privacy budget. The $\gamma$ variant of the grid replaces $\mathcal{P}$ with an encrypted synthetic probe generated by per-client DP-DDPMs and never decrypted at any stage; the binding invariant and the depth budget extend unchanged, with the per-step cost shifted toward the loss-side ciphertext-by-ciphertext multiplications of the ensemble target construction.


\subsubsection{Phase~2: Encrypted Ensemble Target and Encrypted Student SGD}
\label{sec:phase2}

Phase~2 has two components: a one-shot construction of the encrypted ensemble target $\widetilde Y$ at depth at most three multiplicative levels, and a sequence of encrypted SGD steps applied as a linear accumulator on $\langle\theta\rangle$. The student forward pass runs on a plaintext copy of the current weights at every step; only the gradient contribution is encrypted and added to the accumulator. The dominant ciphertext-by-ciphertext cost is therefore the one-shot ensemble target construction, not the per-step update.

\paragraph{$\beta/\lambda$ secure handling.}
\label{sec:ensemble_target}
\par\noindent\textbf{$\beta$-boost without division.} The per-client confidence weights $\alpha_i$ are computed in plaintext at the client (a single scalar each, $\alpha_i = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{P}}[\max\sigma(T_i)]$) and uploaded as encrypted scalars $\langle\alpha_i\rangle$. The server raises each to the power $\beta$ via $\lceil\log_2\beta\rceil$ ciphertext-by-ciphertext multiplications; for the default $\beta{=}2$ this is one multiplication, depth~$+1$. The ensemble target is then constructed using \emph{un-normalised} weights,
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde Y \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^N \langle\alpha_i^\beta\rangle \cdot \langle T_i(\mathcal P)\rangle,
\end{equation}
deliberately omitting the normalisation constant $c = \sum_j \alpha_j^\beta$. At distillation temperature $T'$, the KL identity $\mathrm{KL}(\sigma(c\,Y/T') \| \sigma(S/T')) = \mathrm{KL}(\sigma(Y/(T'/c)) \| \sigma(S/T'))$ shows that the omitted constant is absorbed into an effective temperature $T_{\text{eff}} = T'/c$, so the convergent student is identical to the explicitly-normalised variant. The construction therefore performs no encrypted division, never reveals any per-client confidence in plaintext on the server, and consumes one multiplicative level for the $\beta$-power plus one for the ciphertext-by-ciphertext mixing, for a total of two levels.

\par\noindent\textbf{$\lambda$-boost via uniform-weight per-row variance.} The data-boost factor $r_k = 1 + \lambda V_k$ amplifies the per-row loss contribution where teachers disagree, in a manner that admits a division-free CKKS construction. Per-row variance is estimated with uniform weights,
\begin{equation}
    \langle V_k\rangle \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \langle T_{i,k}\rangle^2 \;-\; \Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_i \langle T_{i,k}\rangle\Big)^{\!2},
\end{equation}
which costs one ciphertext-by-ciphertext square per teacher, one square of the per-row mean, a subtraction (level-free), and a scalar-by-ciphertext multiplication by $\lambda$ (level-free): two multiplicative levels in total. The plaintext $1/N$ factor is absorbed into the scalar-by-ciphertext multiplication. The factor $r_k$ multiplies the per-row contribution to the encrypted loss, adding one further level.

\par\noindent\textbf{Total depth.} Summed across the $\beta$-weighted ensemble and the $\lambda$-boost, the loss-side construction consumes at most three multiplicative levels from raw teacher logits, regardless of $N$. This is the dominant ciphertext-by-ciphertext cost of the entire protocol, incurred once per probe pass rather than once per encrypted SGD step.

\paragraph{Linear-accumulator construction.}
\label{sec:linear_accumulator}
The protocol does not forward-propagate through encrypted student weights. The encrypted student is a \emph{linear accumulator} over teacher-induced gradient contributions,
\begin{equation}
    \langle\theta_E\rangle \;=\; \langle\theta_0^\star\rangle + \sum_t \eta \cdot \langle g_t\rangle,
    \label{eq:linear_accumulator}
\end{equation}
where each $\langle g_t\rangle$ is a per-layer SGD update computed from the encrypted ensemble target $\widetilde Y$ applied to the plaintext student state at step $t$. The student's forward pass at step $t$ runs on the current plaintext copy of the weights; only the resulting gradient contribution is lifted into ciphertext through the encrypted teacher signal. There is no backward chain rule on encrypted intermediate activations, and there is no per-layer depth accumulation across the network's depth.

\par\noindent\textbf{Per-step depth.} The residual carry-over of $\langle\theta\rangle$ across steps is level-free; the plaintext learning-rate factor enters as a scalar-by-ciphertext multiplication (one level); the addition into $\langle\theta\rangle$ is level-free. Combined with the per-step contribution of the loss-side gradient that draws on $\widetilde Y$ (already at depth at most three from §\ref{sec:ensemble_target}), the per-step depth budget is at most three multiplicative levels, with the dominant cost being the one-shot ensemble target rather than any single SGD step.

\par\noindent\textbf{Concrete parameterisation.} At $\log N = 14$ the TenSEAL level chain provides seven multiplicative levels with scale $\approx 2^{40}$ and ring degree~16384. The linear-accumulator construction fits the chain with margin and requires no bootstrapping for any cell of the experimental grid of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, including the deeper LeNet-5 and ViT-tiny student instantiations. The per-step ciphertext-arithmetic latency on a single core is on the order of tens of milliseconds; the per-cell total HE compute is on the order of minutes on commodity hardware, with concrete numbers calibrated against the TenSEAL prototype of Section~\ref{sec:exp_setup}.

\par\noindent\textbf{Released-student inference compatibility.} The student forward pass during training runs on plaintext weights, and the only operator-replacement surface that remains is at \emph{inference} on the released decrypted student. The student architecture is HE-compatible by construction (polynomial activations, affine normalisation in place of LayerNorm or BatchNorm) so that the released $\theta_E$ may be served either in plaintext, or under a fresh single-key CKKS encryption for encrypted inference. The accuracy tax of operator replacement is reported in the experiments section as the triple $(\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{plain-ReLU}}, \mathrm{Acc}_{\text{plain-poly}}, \mathrm{Acc}_{\text{cipher}})$, where the gap between the first two isolates the replacement cost and the gap between the last two isolates the encryption noise cost; both gaps are small under the linear-accumulator construction because polynomial activations enter only at inference time on the released student rather than at every encrypted training step.


\subsubsection{Phase~3: Collective Threshold Key-Switch}
\label{sec:phase3}

The release of the trained student in plaintext is the only stage of the protocol at which the binding invariant of Section~\ref{sec:threat_binding} permits threshold decryption. The mechanism is the collective key-switching primitive of the multiparty CKKS construction of Mouchet et al.~\cite{mouchet2021multiparty}, available in the open-source Lattigo library~\cite{lattigo}.

\par\noindent\textbf{Setup (before Phase~1).} The $N$ clients run a distributed key generation (DKG) protocol with no trusted dealer. Each client $i$ samples a secret-key share $\mathsf{sk}_i$ from the CKKS secret-key distribution and contributes the corresponding public material to the protocol. The DKG output is (i)~a collective public key $\mathsf{pk}$ usable by every party for encryption, (ii)~collective evaluation and rotation keys usable by the server for homomorphic computation, and (iii)~the secret-key shares $\{\mathsf{sk}_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$, retained privately by the clients. The full secret key $\mathsf{sk} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathsf{sk}_i$ is never assembled at any single party. The DKG round is one-time and is amortised across every subsequent protocol run that reuses the same client set.

\par\noindent\textbf{Encrypted upload (Phase~1) and encrypted computation (Phase~2).} Every client encrypts its logit ciphertext $\langle T_i(\mathcal P)\rangle$ and confidence scalar $\langle\alpha_i\rangle$ under the collective public key $\mathsf{pk}$ and uploads to the server. From the server's perspective the resulting ciphertexts behave as ordinary single-key CKKS ciphertexts: the server applies homomorphic additions, ciphertext-by-plaintext multiplications, ciphertext-by-ciphertext multiplications using the collective relinearisation key, and slot rotations using the collective rotation keys. No client interaction is required during the encrypted phase.

\par\noindent\textbf{Collective key-switch (Phase~3).} To release the trained student in plaintext, the server broadcasts the encrypted weights $\langle\theta_E\rangle$ and each client $i$ contributes a key-switching share that re-encrypts $\langle\theta_E\rangle$ from the collective key $\mathsf{pk}$ to a target key $\mathsf{pk}'$ (the all-zero key, yielding plaintext, or a fresh public key whose corresponding secret is held by a designated recipient). Every client's share is computed locally from $\mathsf{sk}_i$ and is masked by a small smudging noise to hide its individual contribution. Combining all $N$ shares yields the re-encrypted ciphertext, from which $\theta_E$ is decrypted. Combining any strict subset of $N{-}1$ shares yields a uniformly random ring element under the RLWE assumption~\cite{lyubashevsky2010ideal}. If the release phase is skipped the student stays encrypted and the server can serve encrypted inference directly.

\par\noindent\textbf{Trust assumption.} Privacy of every client's input holds as long as at least one of the $N$ clients is honest, as guaranteed by the threshold structure of the multiparty CKKS scheme at $t{=}N$. This matches the threat model of Section~\ref{sec:threat_binding}: even if the server colludes with $N{-}1$ clients, the joint view consists of ciphertexts under a key whose remaining share is held by the honest client and is therefore computationally indistinguishable from random.


\subsubsection{Phase~4: Optional Client-Side Personalisation}
\label{sec:phase4}

After the collective key-switch each client holds a plaintext copy of the trained student $\theta_E$. Clients may optionally fine-tune $\theta_E$ on their own private data using standard cross-entropy for a small number of epochs (head-only fine-tuning, freeze body) to obtain a personalised $\theta_{E,i}$. This step requires no further communication with the server or other clients. The personalised student $\theta_{E,i}$ remains on-device and is never uploaded; leakage on $\theta_{E,i}$ therefore reduces to leakage on the released $\theta_E$ by post-processing.


\subsection{Two-Stage Initialisation}
\label{sec:twostage_init}

The $\alpha$ variant of the protocol initialises the encrypted student SGD from a server-side plaintext warm-start on the public probe. Stage~1 trains the student in plaintext on the labelled probe $(\mathcal{P}, y_{\mathcal{P}})$ with cross-entropy for $E_1$ epochs, producing a public-probe-only student $\theta_0^\star$ that knows the probe distribution but carries no client knowledge. Stage~2 encrypts $\theta_0^\star$ as $\langle\theta_0^\star\rangle$ and runs encrypted SGD against the encrypted ensemble target $\widetilde Y$ for $E_2$ epochs, following the linear-accumulator construction of Section~\ref{sec:linear_accumulator}.

\par\noindent\textbf{Hypothesis.} Warm-starting on $\theta_0^\star$ both reduces the number of encrypted SGD steps required for convergence and reduces the magnitude of the encrypted-versus-plaintext discrepancy at a fixed total training budget. The first effect lowers the encrypted compute cost; the second tightens the gap between the published encrypted-domain accuracy and a hypothetical plaintext-domain run of the same protocol.

\par\noindent\textbf{Ablations (A1, A2).} Two ablations isolate the contributions of the construction. (A1)~Random-init Stage~2: $\langle\theta\rangle$ is initialised from a fresh random draw and $E_2'\geq E_2$ is chosen so that the plaintext-equivalent training budget matches; this isolates the warm-start contribution. (A2)~Warm-start without ensemble target: Stage~2 is plaintext SGD on $(\mathcal P, y_{\mathcal P})$ for additional epochs, with no encrypted teacher knowledge; this isolates the encrypted-teacher-knowledge contribution. If A2 were competitive with the full encrypted CFD, the protocol's headline contribution would shift to ``warm-start is enough'' rather than the ensemble construction; the comparison is reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\par\noindent\textbf{Note on the $\gamma$ variant.} In the $\gamma$ variant the probe is encrypted and no plaintext probe labels exist, so warm-start is not defined. The $\gamma$ variant initialises Stage~2 from a shared random seed agreed at protocol start; the encrypted-versus-plaintext gap of the $\gamma$ variant is therefore the floor of what encryption costs the protocol, since no warm-start amortises the cost.

\par\noindent\textbf{Remark on training stability.} The linear-accumulator construction of Section~\ref{sec:linear_accumulator} obviates the encryption-specific training-stability machinery that any forward-encrypted polynomial training would otherwise require. The student forward pass during training runs on plaintext weights and standard backpropagation through standard non-polynomial operators, so encrypted activations never compose across the network's depth and the doubly-exponential composition bound on polynomial deep networks is never invoked during training. Magnitude growth at the released-inference surface is bounded by the standard plaintext-training mechanisms (weight decay, gradient clipping) rather than by an HE-specific penalty term, and the residual operator-replacement cost is reported as the released-student inference-compatibility triple of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\par\noindent\textbf{Comparison.} The per-client traffic is in the same order of magnitude as a single round of FedAvg on a small model and exceeds the plaintext logit upload of the strongest unencrypted one-shot distillation baselines by approximately one order of magnitude due to the CKKS ciphertext expansion factor. This is the cost of the cryptographic guarantee, and it is acceptable for a one-shot protocol that pays this cost exactly once. Iterative encrypted federated learning protocols~\cite{sav2021poseidon, zhang2020batchcrypt} pay the equivalent expansion factor on every round of training, which compounds to several orders of magnitude greater traffic across the hundreds of rounds typical of those protocols.


\subsection{Privacy Analysis}
\label{sec:privacy_proof}

The privacy guarantee of the encrypted CFD protocol is the combination of the binding invariant of Section~\ref{sec:threat_binding} and the IND-CPA security of multiparty CKKS~\cite{cheon2017ckks, mouchet2021multiparty}. Under the binding invariant the only ciphertext ever subjected to threshold decryption is the final student $\theta_E$; under IND-CPA, every other ciphertext the adversary observes (per-client logits, per-client confidences, the ensemble target, the encrypted student accumulator at every step) is computationally indistinguishable from a uniformly random ring element. The adversary's view therefore reduces to a sum of (i)~the released plaintext student $\theta_E$, (ii)~plaintext metadata (the public probe, ciphertext sizes and counts, protocol timing), and (iii)~ciphertexts indistinguishable from random. The released-student leakage is the unavoidable lower bound of any collaborative-training protocol, and the cryptographic instantiation holds the adversary's view at exactly this floor.

The defence against the all-zeros amplification attack is the structural admission of two independent differential-privacy knobs as described in Section~\ref{sec:threat_binding}: DP-SGD-trained teachers at a per-client budget $(\varepsilon_T, \delta_T)$, and an optional per-row Gaussian noise on the logit upload at budget $(\varepsilon_P, \delta_P)$. By the post-processing theorem the released student inherits the composition of these budgets under the standard Gaussian-DP RDP accountant; the present methodology's claim is structural, that the protocol admits both knobs while preserving the binding invariant, with concrete budget values reported in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.


\subsection{Architecture-Specific Instantiations}
\label{sec:architecture}

The encrypted CFD protocol of Section~\ref{sec:phase2} is parametric in the student architecture: any HE-compatible student whose released-inference forward pass can be expressed in CKKS-compatible operations is admissible. The student forward pass during training runs on a plaintext copy of the weights and uses standard non-polynomial operators (ReLU, BatchNorm, GELU, LayerNorm), so the architectural design surface lives entirely at the released-inference boundary.

\subsubsection{Convolutional Networks}
\label{sec:arch_cnn}

The convolutional instantiation uses a LeNet-5 student as the default for the headline grid, with PolyResNet-style replacements applied only at the released-inference surface: ReLU is replaced by a learnable degree-2 polynomial $\mathrm{PolyAct}(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ initialised near-linearly~\cite{baruch2022methodology, garimella2021sisyphus}, BatchNorm is folded into a per-channel affine $\mathrm{ChannelScale}(x) = \gamma\odot x + \beta$ with parameters frozen at release time, and MaxPool is replaced by an identity operation with spatial downsampling handled by stride-2 convolutions. The released student therefore costs one multiplicative level per polynomial activation at encrypted inference and zero levels for the affine and convolutional operators. This is the operator-replacement surface that the released-student inference-compatibility tax of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} measures; it is decoupled from the training-time computation by the linear-accumulator construction of Section~\ref{sec:linear_accumulator}.

\subsubsection{Transformer and Vision Transformer Architectures}
\label{sec:arch_transformer}

The transformer instantiation uses a ViT-tiny student fine-tuned from a publicly pre-trained ViT-B/32 backbone. The released-inference surface replaces GELU by a degree-4 polynomial approximation (PolyGELU) fitted over the working dynamic range and replaces LayerNorm by an AffineNorm primitive that retains the learnable per-element scale and bias but elides the division by the running standard deviation. As in the convolutional case, these replacements affect only released-inference latency and the operator-replacement tax measured in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}; the student forward pass during encrypted training runs on plaintext weights using the standard GELU and LayerNorm operators.
